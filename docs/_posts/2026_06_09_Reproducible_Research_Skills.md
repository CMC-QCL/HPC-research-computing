# Developing Reproducible Research Skills Through the QCL Summer Bootcamp

<img src="{{ '/images/BlogPost_2026_06_09_v2.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="BlogPost_2026_06_09_v2" class="img-67-centered" />

The Murty Sunak Quantitative and Computing Lab (QCL) recently concluded its Summer Research Computing Bootcamp, a week-long series designed to help students develop practical computational skills for research projects across disciplines.

Held from June 1–5 in the QCL, the bootcamp brought together students working on a wide range of summer research projects. Whether participants were analyzing datasets, writing code, creating maps, or managing research workflows, the sessions focused on helping students build skills that support efficient, organized, and reproducible research.

## The Importance of Reproducible Research

A central theme throughout the week was **reproducible research** -- the practice of organizing data, code, and documentation so that research results can be recreated and verified by others.

Reproducible workflows help researchers maintain transparency, improve collaboration, and ensure that projects remain understandable and usable long after they are completed. These practices are increasingly important across disciplines and are becoming a standard expectation in academic research and industry settings alike.

For students, adopting reproducible workflows early in their academic careers helps create stronger research habits, reduces time spent troubleshooting past work, and makes collaboration with faculty advisors and fellow researchers more effective.

## What Students Learned

Throughout the week, participants were introduced to a collection of tools and techniques that support modern research workflows.

### GitHub and Version Control

Students learned the fundamentals of version control using GitHub, including how to track changes, manage project files, and collaborate with others. These skills help researchers maintain organized project histories and facilitate teamwork.

### Python for Research

The Python session introduced participants to programming fundamentals, virtual environments, and practical coding workflows. Students also explored how AI-assisted coding tools can be incorporated into research projects while maintaining good coding practices.

### R for Data Analysis

Participants learned how R can be used for data analysis, scripting, and visualization. The workshop covered common workflows for cleaning, analyzing, and presenting research data.

### Geographic Information Systems (GIS)

The GIS workshop explored spatial data analysis and mapping techniques. Students gained exposure to tools that allow researchers to visualize geographic patterns and answer location-based research questions.

### High-Performance Computing (HPC) for Research

The final session introduced students to research computing resources that extend beyond the capabilities of a personal computer. Topics included remote computing, SSH access, Bash fundamentals, and batch job submission, providing students with a foundation for utilizing high-performance computing resources in future projects.

## Building Skills Across Disciplines

One of the strengths of the bootcamp was the diversity of research interests represented among participants. Students from quantitative, qualitative, experimental, theoretical, and interdisciplinary backgrounds engaged with the material and explored how computational tools can enhance their research workflows.

The skills introduced throughout the week are applicable well beyond traditional data science projects. Reproducible research practices can support everything from text analysis and archival research to laboratory experiments and large-scale computational studies.

## Looking Ahead

We appreciate everyone who participated in the Summer Research Computing Bootcamp and contributed to a week of learning and collaboration. It was exciting to see students actively engage with new tools and begin incorporating reproducible research practices into their summer projects.

QCL remains committed to supporting student and faculty research through training, consultation, and access to computational resources. We look forward to continuing to build a strong research computing community across the Claremont Colleges and supporting the innovative work being conducted this summer and beyond.
