# QCL Presents at CLAC 2026: Integrating Research Computing into a Consortium-First IT Model

<img src="{{ '/images/BlogPost_2026_06_11.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="BlogPost_2026_06_11" class="img-67-centered" />

The Murty Sunak Quantitative and Computing Lab (QCL) was pleased to present Session 17, *Integrating Research Computing into a Consortium-First IT Model*, at CLAC 2026. The session brought together higher education professionals interested in exploring how liberal arts college consortia can expand research computing support while leveraging existing shared IT infrastructure.

Presented by Dr. Jeho Park, Daniel Perez, and Dr. Vanessa Arias Casillas, the session examined the growing demand for advanced computing resources driven by data-intensive research, artificial intelligence, and machine learning initiatives across disciplines.

Participants explored practical strategies for aligning research computing services with consortium-wide infrastructure and collaboration models. Topics included consultation workflows, faculty and student training, onboarding approaches, partnerships with regional high-performance computing (HPC) centers, and the role of research computing ambassadors in connecting researchers to advanced cyberinfrastructure resources.

The presentation also highlighted the unique experiences of supporting research computing within The Claremont Colleges consortium. Through interactive discussions and audience engagement activities, attendees shared perspectives on governance models, cross-campus collaboration, and approaches for helping faculty and students navigate increasingly complex computing ecosystems.

We were excited by the thoughtful discussions and enthusiastic participation throughout the session. As research computing continues to evolve, collaborations like those fostered at CLAC provide valuable opportunities to share ideas, learn from peers, and strengthen support for computational research across higher education.

Thank you to everyone who attended Session 17 and contributed to the conversation. We look forward to continuing these discussions and collaborating with colleagues across institutions to make advanced computing resources more accessible, inclusive, and impactful.
